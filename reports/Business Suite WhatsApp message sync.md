# Business Suite replies escape WhatsApp webhooks

No Meta-documented mechanism is confirmed to deliver the text of a WhatsApp reply typed in the Meta Business Suite inbox to a third-party Cloud API app, and no official API returns a message body after the fact. The four fields the app already subscribes to cannot close the gap: `messages` carries only status for outbound messages, `smb_message_echoes` is documented only for the WhatsApp Business app and supported linked devices, and `history` and `smb_app_state_sync` are one-time onboarding syncs. The single official lead is the newer `standby` webhook field from Conversation Routing, whose `standby.message_echoes` payload carries the full send-time body of messages sent by "another responder" on the number. Meta documents it around Meta Business Agent and never names the Business Suite inbox as a responder, so whether it covers this case is untested. The only way to guarantee today that every reply shows with its text is to stop WhatsApp replies from being sent in Business Suite at all: staff reply in the app itself or in the WhatsApp Business app (which echoes with content), ideally enforced by disconnecting WhatsApp from the Business Suite inbox. Unofficial routes (Baileys-style libraries, scraping business.facebook.com) violate Meta's terms and put the business's main number and accounts at risk. The recommended order is: show a placeholder for text-less replies now, run a one-hour `standby` test, and, if that test fails, remove WhatsApp from the Business Suite inbox.

## The four subscribed fields cannot carry Business Suite text by design

The behaviour the app sees today is what Meta's documentation predicts, not a bug in the integration. **The `messages` field covers inbound user messages plus "the status of messages sent by a business to a WhatsApp user"**, so an outbound message is only ever represented there by sent, delivered and read events ([Webhooks overview](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/webhooks/overview)). The status payload contains message ID, status, timestamp, recipient, pricing and errors, with no body field ([Status webhook reference](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/webhooks/reference/messages/status)).

**`smb_message_echoes` is scoped strictly to the WhatsApp Business app and supported companion devices.** Meta's reference says it notifies of messages "sent via the WhatsApp Business app or a" companion ("linked") device, and the coexistence guide describes it as covering "any new messages your client sends with the WhatsApp Business app after onboarding" ([smb_message_echoes reference](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/webhooks/reference/smb_message_echoes); [Onboarding WhatsApp Business app users](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/embedded-signup/onboarding-business-app-users)). All companion clients are supported except WhatsApp for Windows and WhatsApp for WearOS, and messages from unsupported clients trigger no webhook ([Onboarding WhatsApp Business app users](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/embedded-signup/onboarding-business-app-users)). Neither page mentions Meta Business Suite or any inbox. The exclusion is therefore by omission, not by an explicit sentence, and 360dialog, Telnyx and Gupshup describe the field the same way ([360dialog](https://docs.360dialog.com/docs/waba-basics/phone-numbers/coexistence/coexistence-webhooks); [Telnyx](https://developers.telnyx.com/docs/messaging/whatsapp/coexistence/webhooks); [Gupshup](https://docs.gupshup.io/docs/coexistence-events-webhooks)).

Why Business Suite sends fall outside the echo is an inference, but a well-supported one. Meta states that Business app messages "are not subject to the customer service window" and sit outside Cloud API pricing ([Onboarding WhatsApp Business app users](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/embedded-signup/onboarding-business-app-users)). The statuses the app receives for Business Suite replies carry a `pricing` object, which Business app sends never have. Business Suite therefore sends through Meta's server-side platform path, like a separate platform connection, not as a linked device. Third-party guides support this picture: the WhatsApp Business app lists a connection literally named "Meta Business Suite Inbox" under Settings > Account > Business Platform, next to partner integrations ([dev.to, 2 Nov 2026](https://dev.to/martinmunozb/error-3441034-de-whatsapp-business-coexistence-como-lo-resolvi-al-desvincular-meta-business-suite-49m5); [ChatDaddy, 9 Jun 2026](https://help.chatdaddy.tech/article/how-to-disconnect-whatsapp-business-app-from-meta-inbox-to-link-with-chatdaddy)). Meta itself documents nothing about the Business Suite inbox's WhatsApp transport.

One third-party claim needs to be discarded. Dualhook's documentation lists a standalone `message_echoes` field for "messages the business sent through the Cloud API" from any tool ([Dualhook](https://dualhook.com/docs/webhook-smb-message-echoes)). **Meta documents no such WABA field**: it is absent from the overview field table, and the reference URL for it returns "Page Not Found" ([Webhooks overview](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/webhooks/overview); [message_echoes reference, 404](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/webhooks/reference/message_echoes.md)). `message_echoes` exists as a real webhook event only on the Messenger Platform, and as an array name inside the `smb_message_echoes` and `standby` payloads ([Messenger message_echoes](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/messenger-platform/webhooks/webhook-events/message-echoes)).

## `standby` is the only official field that could carry the text

Conversation Routing adds a `standby` webhook field that notifies an app "about conversation activity on a WhatsApp Business phone number when another responder owns the thread", with three sub-objects: `standby.messages`, `standby.message_echoes` and `standby.statuses` ([Standby webhooks](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/webhooks/reference/standby/)). The echo is exactly what the owner is missing: **"When another responder on the number — such as Meta Business Agent — sends an outbound message to a WhatsApp user, your app receives an 'echo' of that message"**, carrying the send-time parameters as passed to the Send Message API, including `text.body` ([Standby webhooks](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/webhooks/reference/standby/)).

Three documented conditions apply. The app must subscribe to `standby` under WhatsApp > Configuration > Webhook fields. Then "subscribing alone is not enough — the business must also grant your app standby visibility" in its routing configuration in Meta Business Suite; standby is off by default and an app cannot grant it to itself ([Standby webhooks](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/webhooks/reference/standby/)). Finally, the standby option only appears after the business accepts the "Meta Business Agents and Platform Terms of Service" ([Standby partners](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/conversation-routing/standby-partners/)). Meta's Business Agent setup guide pairs `standby` with a third field, `messaging_handovers`, which reports changes of thread control ([Meta Business Agent setup](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/meta-business-agent/get-started)).

**Whether a human reply typed in the Business Suite inbox counts as "another responder" is not documented anywhere.** Meta defines a responder as "any party that can hold a role on the WhatsApp account and receive routed traffic", which includes third-party partner apps, Meta apps and first-party AI responders, but it names neither the Business Suite inbox nor the WhatsApp Business app, and it does not address coexistence numbers ([Conversation Routing overview](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/conversation-routing/overview/)). Every Meta example for the echo is Meta Business Agent. 360dialog confirms the mechanism for agent replies ("Meta delivers every agent reply to your app as an echo on standby.message_echoes") and adds that Meta documents no inbox behaviour, so providers should "test on an allowlisted number" ([360dialog](https://360dialog.com/blog/bsp-integration-meta-business-agent-whatsapp/)). No public report of anyone testing this against Business Suite replies was found.

The field also comes with costs that must be understood before switching it on. Exactly one responder owns a thread, **"only the thread owner may send Service messages"**, and an app receives no standby events for threads it owns or messages it sent itself ([Standby webhooks](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/webhooks/reference/standby/)). Standby partners cannot send service messages unless they are the designated escalation partner, whose reply counts as an implicit take of the thread ([Standby partners](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/conversation-routing/standby-partners/)). Without a routing configuration every connected responder receives every message, routing needs more than one capable responder, and the configuration is deleted automatically if fewer than two remain ([Conversation Routing overview](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/conversation-routing/overview/)). A careless routing setup can therefore break the app's own ability to reply. Unknown and not found in any source: whether routing is offered for coexistence numbers at all, in which countries, without enabling Meta Business Agent, and when the feature launched. The Conversation Routing "Get started" and "Thread control" pages were not read during this research.

## No API returns a message body after the fact

The fallback idea of fetching the text later by `wamid` does not work. The Cloud API messages reference documents only `POST /{PHONE_NUMBER_ID}/messages`, and the platform overview states that message content reaches a business by webhook ([Cloud API messages reference](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/cloud-api/reference/messages); [About the platform](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/about-the-platform)). Meta does operate a Message History Events API, `GET /{Message-History-ID}/events`, but its fields are `delivery_status`, `occurrence_timestamp`, `error_description`, `application`, `webhook_update_state` and `webhook_uri`. **It returns delivery metadata only, with no text field**, so it adds nothing to the status webhooks already received ([Message History Events API](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/reference/message-history/whatsapp-business-message-history-events-api)). Meta keeps messages for at most 30 days for retransmission and never describes that store as retrievable ([Data Privacy & Security](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/data-privacy-and-security)). Meta never writes "there is no history API" in so many words; the conclusion rests on the absence of any such endpoint in the reference and on consistent developer reports ([GitHub discussion](https://github.com/gabrieldwight/Whatsapp-Business-Cloud-Api-Net/discussions/63)).

The Graph surface behind the Business Suite unified inbox is closed for WhatsApp as well. On `GET /{page-id}/conversations` the `platform` parameter is "enum {INSTAGRAM, MESSENGER}" in the current v27.0 reference, and the Conversations API guide is scoped to Messenger and Instagram ([Page Conversations reference](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/graph-api/reference/page/conversations/); [Conversations API](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/messenger-platform/conversations)). The Marketing Messages API and analytics endpoints are send and metrics only ([MM API changelog](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/marketing-messages/changelog)). The 2025 and 2026 changelog entries reviewed add webhook-side features (coexistence launch on 11 Feb 2025, edit and revoke echoes on 25 Nov 2025, business-scoped user IDs) and no retrieval endpoint, although the changelog fetch was probably truncated after mid-2026 ([WhatsApp changelog](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/changelog/)).

History sync is the one official channel that delivers bodies of past messages, and it is a migration tool. `POST /{phone-number-id}/smb_app_data` with `sync_type: history` must be called within 24 hours of onboarding, and **"you can only perform this step once"**; to repeat it "the customer must first offboard, then complete the Embedded Signup flow again" ([Onboarding WhatsApp Business app users](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/embedded-signup/onboarding-business-app-users)). The resulting `history` webhooks cover messages sent or received within 180 days of onboarding and include `id`, `text.body` and a status ([history webhook reference](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/webhooks/reference/history/)). Whether a re-onboarding sync would contain Business Suite replies is speculation: it depends on whether those replies land in the phone app's chat store, which no source confirms. Offboarding the number to find out is disruptive, backfills only, and does nothing for future replies.

## Workarounds split into safe-but-manual and risky-and-brittle

Shared-inbox vendors do not solve this problem; they avoid it. No vendor reviewed claims to sync replies typed in the Business Suite inbox, and none names it in its coexistence limitations. Several instead treat "Meta Business Suite Inbox" as a competing connection to remove: ChatDaddy instructs users to open WhatsApp Business > Settings > Account > Business Platform, select the connection and disconnect it, and a developer write-up from 2 Nov 2026 used the same path, warning that it stops Inbox handling of WhatsApp and must not be confused with "Delete WhatsApp account" or "Remove from business portfolio" ([ChatDaddy](https://help.chatdaddy.tech/article/how-to-disconnect-whatsapp-business-app-from-meta-inbox-to-link-with-chatdaddy); [dev.to](https://dev.to/martinmunozb/error-3441034-de-whatsapp-business-coexistence-como-lo-resolvi-al-desvincular-meta-business-suite-49m5)). These steps are third-party only; no Meta Help Center article was retrieved, and menu names vary by app version and language. That Messenger and Instagram stay in the Business Suite inbox after the WhatsApp connection is removed is a reasonable inference (they hang on the Page and Instagram assets), not a sourced fact. respond.io states that a coexistence number "can be connected to only one partner at a time" ([respond.io](https://respond.io/help/whatsapp/whatsapp-coexistence)), which conflicts with the owner's setup where Business Suite and the custom app both work; the notes offer no verified explanation.

| Approach | What it delivers | Evidence status | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reply only in the custom app or the WhatsApp Business app | Full text, always (own sends, or `smb_message_echoes`) | Confirmed by Meta docs | None; depends on staff discipline |
| Disconnect "Meta Business Suite Inbox" from WhatsApp | Removes the gap at its source | Reported by third parties; effect on Messenger/Instagram and ads untested | Low; do not disconnect the custom app's own entry |
| `standby` field with standby visibility | Full text of other responders' sends | Mechanism confirmed by Meta; coverage of Business Suite untested | Thread-control side effects on the app's own sending |
| Placeholder from the status webhook | Presence of a reply, no text | Follows from observed behaviour | None |
| Restrict staff permissions on the WhatsApp asset | Unknown | Speculation; no source shows a WhatsApp inbox reply permission | None |
| Re-onboarding for a fresh history sync | One-time backfill at best | Sync limits confirmed; inclusion of Business Suite replies is speculation | Disruptive offboarding |
| Manual "Export chat" on the phone | Text without `wamid`, per chat | Reported; only useful if replies appear on the phone (unconfirmed) | None; high manual effort |
| Baileys, whatsapp-web.js or similar linked-device library | Text, if replies sync to linked devices (unconfirmed) | Third-party reports | High: violates WhatsApp terms, ban risk on the main number |
| Scraping business.facebook.com | Text while the DOM stays stable | Feasible in principle | High: prohibited without Meta's written permission |

The remaining ideas do not hold up. No Business Suite export, email notification or automation carrying WhatsApp reply text was found, and Zapier and Make sit on the same Cloud API webhooks, so they see nothing the app does not; Make's module reportedly does not even expose `smb_message_echoes` ([Make community](https://community.make.com/t/whatsapp-coexistence-watch-events-does-not-expose-smb-message-echoes/114713)). Chat export exists only in the mobile apps ([Periskope](https://periskope.app/content/whatsapp-export-chat)). Unofficial linked-device libraries violate WhatsApp's terms and risk suspension, and a poisoned npm fork of Baileys has stolen auth tokens ([Zylos research, Jan 2026](https://zylos.ai/zh/research/2026-01-26-whatsapp-api-automation)); a ban would take down the Cloud API side of the same number. Vendor claims of zero-warning bans since late 2025 are unverified against Meta ([ChatDaddy](https://help.chatdaddy.tech/article/ban-type-distribution)). Scraping the inbox falls under Meta's Automated Data Collection Terms: "You will not engage in Automated Data Collection without first obtaining Meta's express written permission", and Meta has sued makers of data-collecting browser extensions ([Meta terms](https://www.facebook.com/legal/automated_data_collection_terms); [Meta newsroom, 2022](https://about.fb.com/news/2022/07/actions-against-scraping-for-hire/)). Both routes are ruled out.

## Recommended order: placeholder, standby test, then close the Business Suite door

**First priority, this week: make the gap visible.** The app already receives the `wamid`, recipient and timestamp of every Business Suite reply through status webhooks. It should render these as a reply bubble with a note such as "sent from Meta Business Suite, text not available" in place of dropping them. This costs little, carries no risk, shows the owner how often the problem occurs, and gives a slot to fill if text arrives later through `standby`. In parallel, a team rule (reply to WhatsApp only in the app or the WhatsApp Business app) removes most new cases immediately, because both of those paths are documented to deliver the text.

**Second priority: test the `standby` lead, time-boxed to about an hour.** The test should run in this sequence, stopping at the first step that fails. The first step is a read-only availability check: open the Meta app's dashboard under WhatsApp > Configuration > Webhook fields and see whether `standby` and `messaging_handovers` are offered (`GET /{app-id}/subscriptions` shows the current subscriptions). If `standby` is not listed, the feature is not available to this app and the test ends. The second step is to subscribe to `standby` and `messaging_handovers` and to make the webhook endpoint log every raw payload unfiltered, including unknown `field` values, so nothing is silently dropped by the current parser. The third step is a baseline: send one WhatsApp reply from the Business Suite inbox to a test contact before touching any routing setting, and record what arrives. Subscription alone is documented as insufficient, so the expected result is the status events only; anything else is a finding. The fourth step is to look in Meta Business Suite for the Conversation Routing configuration of the WhatsApp account and note, before changing anything, which responders are listed. **This is the decisive observation: if the Business Suite inbox is not listed as a responder, `standby.message_echoes` will not cover it, and the test ends without any configuration change.** Reaching this screen requires accepting the Meta Business Agents and Platform Terms of Service, which is a decision for the owner, not a technical step. The fifth step, only if the inbox is listed, is to grant the app standby visibility while keeping the app able to send, as primary responder or escalation partner, and then to send three test messages: one from Business Suite, one from the app, one from the WhatsApp Business app. Success means a webhook with `field: "standby"` containing `message_echoes` whose `id` matches the `wamid` in the corresponding status events and whose `message.text.body` holds the reply text. The app's own send must still succeed and the Business app send must still arrive as `smb_message_echoes`. The sixth step is a regression check on a real conversation: after a Business Suite reply, the customer answers, and staff reply from the app. If that app reply fails with a thread-ownership error, the routing configuration must be removed again, which the documentation describes as returning to the state where every responder receives every message. The whole test should run outside peak hours and with one test contact, because the thread-control side effects on a coexistence number are undocumented.

**Third priority, if the test fails or the feature is unavailable: remove WhatsApp from the Business Suite inbox.** On the phone that holds the number, WhatsApp Business > Settings > Account > Business Platform lists the connections; only the entry named "Meta Business Suite Inbox" is disconnected, never the entry for the owner's own app. Before doing this, the owner should confirm on the phone which entries exist, and afterwards verify that Messenger and Instagram still work in Business Suite, that the app still sends and receives, and that any Click-to-WhatsApp ads and the Page's WhatsApp button still behave, since none of these effects is documented. This is the only measure that makes "every reply always shows with its text" true by construction, because every remaining send path is one Meta documents as delivering content to the app.

**Not recommended at any priority:** re-onboarding the number for a history sync, unofficial linked-device libraries, and scraping the Business Suite web inbox. For the handful of past replies that matter, staff can copy the text into the app by hand from the Business Suite thread.

## Conclusion

The owner's requirement and Meta's architecture pull in opposite directions: Meta treats each sending surface as the keeper of its own message content and shares only status across surfaces, so a complete inbox is built by controlling where staff type, not by fetching what they typed elsewhere. `standby` is the first sign that Meta is willing to share outbound content between responders on one number, which makes it worth one careful test, but it was designed for AI-agent handover and may never include a human inbox.

The practical consequence is that the app should become the single place for WhatsApp replies, with the WhatsApp Business app as the documented second path, and Business Suite kept for Messenger and Instagram. If the `standby` test succeeds, that result is new information not found in any public source reviewed here and should be re-verified after each Meta platform update, since the behaviour would rest on an undocumented reading of "responder".
